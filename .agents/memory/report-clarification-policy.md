---
name: Report clarification policy
description: Why report contradictions and prior verification must remain distinct from later human corrections.
---

Preserve resident statements and earlier clarification evidence separately from the current draft. An AI check identifies inconsistencies; it does not resolve them. Missing form inputs must not erase contradictions found within the narrative.

**Why:** CivicClear is a human-reviewed class prototype. Choosing one conflicting statement silently—or treating an empty form field as more important than detected contradictory statements—would hide the uncertainty that the reviewer needs to see.

**How to apply:** Keep conflict-specific questions even when a corresponding form input is unknown. Human corrections require a clear value and evidence, while original findings remain available.

Verification applies to the values actually reviewed, not permanently to a field or report. Material edits after clarification invalidate the affected verification and any prior approval; renewed human review must preserve earlier evidence.

**Why:** Otherwise a report can retain high readiness and approval after its verified address, urgency, or department changes to a contradictory value.

**How to apply:** Use the same unresolved-information predicate for readiness, department display, and approval. Never let generic draft editing act as a shortcut around evidence-backed clarification.

Preserve the resident's requested urgency as source input, but let reported high-consequence safety indicators support a separate elevated AI recommendation. Possible hazardous-material concerns remain possible, not confirmed, and must stay visible alongside missing-address warnings.

**Why:** Copying a low resident-selected urgency over a safety assessment, or replacing an unverified hazard with a generic missing-value label, hides the concern a human needs to prioritize.

**How to apply:** Keep source quotes and the original recommendation available after human review. Elevating urgency does not resolve uncertainty, authorize routing, or make the final decision; a human must verify the reported concern.
